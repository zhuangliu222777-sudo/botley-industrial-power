Industry applications

Protect the process, not only the equipment.

Different loads fail in different ways. We connect grid conditions, equipment behavior and continuity targets to the right combination of regulation, conversion, backup and power quality control.

Eight served industries

Power strategies shaped by the production environment.

The sectors below reflect the customer coverage documented in Botley's company profile.

01

Printing & converting

Multi-motor presses, registration controls and finishing lines are vulnerable to voltage fluctuation and short interruptions.

Typical risks
Drive alarms, registration loss, scrap and restart delays
Relevant systems
Voltage stabilizers, transformers, UPS
02

CNC & machine tools

Precision machining relies on stable power for servo drives, CNC controls, spindle systems and imported auxiliaries.

Typical risks
Control resets, tool errors, nuisance trips and dimensional drift
Relevant systems
Integrated stabilizer/transformers, harmonic filters
03

Steel & heavy industry

Large motors, rectifiers and rapidly changing loads demand robust regulation and power quality planning.

Typical risks
Voltage sag, low power factor, harmonics and thermal stress
Relevant systems
High-capacity stabilizers, transformers, harmonic filters
04

Shipbuilding

Fabrication equipment, dockside supplies and mixed workshop loads require dependable voltage adaptation and isolation.

Typical risks
Variable site supply, mixed voltages and harsh duty cycles
Relevant systems
Dry-type transformers, stabilizers, integrated systems
05

Automotive manufacturing

Automated lines combine robots, welders, conveyors and controls with demanding transient and continuity requirements.

Typical risks
Line stoppage, controller faults, voltage dips and nonlinear loads
Relevant systems
Stabilizers, UPS, transformers, harmonic filtering
06

Telecom & broadcast

Network and broadcast infrastructure needs conditioned power, ride-through capability and maintainable bypass architecture.

Typical risks
Service interruption, battery depletion and maintenance exposure
Relevant systems
Online UPS, PLG bypass cabinets, harmonic filters
07

Medical equipment

Imaging, laboratory and treatment systems require stable, clean power matched to equipment and facility requirements.

Typical risks
Diagnostic interruption, sensitive electronics and data loss
Relevant systems
UPS, isolation transformers, precision stabilizers
08

Aerospace & research

Test benches, laboratories and imported instruments depend on controlled voltage and frequency conditions.

Typical risks
Non-repeatable tests, incompatible grids and supply disturbance
Relevant systems
Frequency converters, transformers, regulated supplies

Machine tools01

Imported CNC line with incompatible and unstable plant voltage

Challenge

The machine requires a different operating voltage while the incoming supply moves outside the preferred tolerance.

Configuration

SBW/SG integrated stabilizer-transformer sized for running load, startup demand and future margin.

Engineering focus

Input range, output accuracy, phase sequence, bypass arrangement and protection coordination.

Export testing02

Production test station for multiple destination-market grids

Challenge

One line must validate products at different voltages and frequencies without changing utility service.

Configuration

Programmable AC frequency converter with selectable phase arrangement and defined output range.

Engineering focus

Test sequence, peak load, frequency range, THD, operator interface and protection settings.

Telecom infrastructure03

Critical load requiring continuity during UPS maintenance

Challenge

The protected load must remain energized while UPS equipment is isolated for planned service.

Configuration

Online UPS coordinated with a PLG maintenance bypass and project-specific isolation arrangement.

Engineering focus

Bypass interlocking, phase configuration, transfer procedure, fault protection and maintainability.

Heavy manufacturing04

Plant distribution affected by nonlinear production loads

Challenge

Drives and rectifiers introduce harmonic current and reactive demand into the distribution system.

Configuration

Tuned harmonic filter cabinet selected from measured load data and the site's target harmonics.

Engineering focus

Harmonic survey, resonance risk, switching stages, reactive compensation and thermal conditions.

Engineering inputs

Turn your operating data into a project brief.

A suitable solution starts with the actual equipment and its supply. Nameplate values are useful, but operating measurements and load behavior often reveal the decisive factors.

Discuss an application →
  • Utility voltage range and frequency
  • Single-line diagram and existing protection
  • Load list, simultaneity and startup current
  • Measured harmonics or power factor, if available
  • Required autonomy, redundancy and bypass strategy
  • Ambient temperature, humidity, altitude and enclosure needs
